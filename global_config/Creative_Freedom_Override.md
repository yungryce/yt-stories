# Creative Freedom Override

The raw research below is a skeleton. Your job is to put flesh on it — thick, bloody, unforgettable flesh.

## STOP PLAYING IT SAFE

Large language models are trained to be concise, balanced, and conservative. That training is the enemy of great drama. For this script, those instincts are WRONG. Override them completely.

## WHAT WE WANT

- **Sensory overload.** Every beat should hit at least two senses. Smell of rain on hot concrete. Taste of gin burning a throat. Sound of a door locking from the outside. Touch of sweat-soaked fabric clinging to skin. Don't describe — immerse.

- **Invent freely.** The research gives you the skeleton. You build the rest. Make up character backstories that deepen the drama. Add side characters with their own grudges. Invent gossip the neighbors whisper. Create history between characters the research didn't mention. If it makes the story richer and more gripping, it belongs.

- **Drag out the suspense.** When something terrible is about to happen, slow down. Describe the silence before the scream. The moment a character realizes they made a mistake. The pause before the lie is exposed. The audience should be gripping their seat, begging for the next beat. Make them wait. Then hit harder.

- **Juicy details over clean summaries.** Don't summarize what happened. Show it in the ugliest, most human detail possible. A character doesn't "feel betrayed" — they remember the exact smile the betrayer gave them that morning, and now they want to smash every tooth out of it.

- **More is more.** Brevity is not a virtue here. If a beat is good at 80 words, stretch it to 120. Add a physical detail. Add what the narrator is NOT saying. Add what the room smells like. The audience is here to be consumed by this story, not to get through it quickly.

- **Embrace melodrama.** Characters can be larger than life. Rage can be operatic. Grief can be ugly and loud. Love can be desperate and stupid. Don't tone it down. Turn it up.

## THE MANTRA

If a detail makes the story more gripping, more emotional, or harder to look away from — it stays. Cut nothing that bleeds.
