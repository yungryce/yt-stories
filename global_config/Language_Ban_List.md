# Language Ban List — Words & Phrasing That Kill The Story

If the narrator sounds like a textbook, a critic, or a TED Talk, the viewer leaves. This is entertainment — visceral, direct, emotional. Keep it simple. Keep it punchy.

## BANNED WORDS (never use these in narration)

- delve, delve into
- testament (as in "a testament to")
- moreover, furthermore, nevertheless, nonetheless
- revolutionize, transformative, paradigm
- nuanced, dichotomy, juxtaposition
- underscore, highlight (as in "this underscores...")
- ultimately, fundamentally, essentially
- notion, construct, framework
- arguably, ostensibly, purportedly
- poignant, profound, evocative
- consequently, accordingly, thus
- intricate, multifaceted, multidimensional

## BANNED PHRASING (cut on sight)

- "It is worth noting that..."
- "One could argue that..."
- "This serves as a reminder that..."
- "What makes this particularly [adjective] is..."
- "In the context of..."
- "The significance of this cannot be overstated"
- "At its core, this is a story about..."
- "This represents a shift in..."
- "On a deeper level..."
- "Beneath the surface..."
- "In many ways..."
- "It becomes clear that..."

## BANNED TONES (rewrite entirely)

- **Wikipedia summary voice** — neutral, clinical, balanced, detached
- **Academic critique voice** — analytical, qualifying, hedging, thesis-like
- **Marketing copy voice** — hype, jargon, "engagement"-speak, buzzwords
- **TED Talk voice** — inspirational abstraction, big-idea framing, clever pivots

## THE RULE

If you wouldn't whisper it to someone sitting next to you in a dark room, cut it.

No indirect inferences. No thesis statements. No literary analysis disguised as narration.

Characters feel. Characters scream. Characters bleed. The language does the same.
